% Abstract
\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM) has emerged as a powerful computational technique for simulating fluid dynamics, offering significant advantages over traditional Navier-Stokes solvers for complex geometries and interfacial flows. However, the standard Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK) collision operator suffers from stability limitations at high Reynolds numbers, restricting its applicability to practical engineering problems. This limitation stems from the method's fundamental disconnect between viscosity and stability parameters.

This thesis presents a comprehensive implementation and validation of the Entropic Lattice Boltzmann Method (ELBM), which addresses the stability problem by enforcing the discrete H-theorem during collision. By guaranteeing that entropy does not increase ($\mathrm{d}H/\mathrm{d}t \leq 0$), ELBM achieves unconditional thermodynamic stability regardless of Reynolds number, providing a mathematically grounded alternative to heuristic stability fixes.

We validate ELBM across an extensive suite of test cases: (1) \textbf{Analytical cases}: Couette flow, Hagen-Poiseuille flow, and Taylor-Green vortex decay with L2 errors below 0.1\%; (2) \textbf{Rectangular channel flows}: at Re $\approx 10$, 100, and 1000, replicating Yu's 2021 thesis results; (3) \textbf{Benchmark flows}: lid-driven cavity (Re = 100, 1000) and flow past a circular cylinder; (4) \textbf{Multiphase flows}: static droplet validation using the Shan-Chen pseudo-potential model; (5) \textbf{Active matter}: preliminary investigation of self-propelled particle coupling with ELBM. All simulations demonstrate ELBM's superior stability and accuracy compared to standard BGK, particularly at high Reynolds numbers where BGK fails completely.

Performance analysis reveals that ELBM is approximately 7-9 times slower than BGK due to iterative computation of the entropy-preserving parameter $\alpha$. This computational overhead is justified for high-Re flows where BGK becomes unstable. We provide detailed cost-benefit analysis and computational bottleneck profiling to guide method selection.

The framework is designed with extensibility in mind, featuring a header-only C++ implementation with template-based lattice structures enabling straightforward extension to new collision operators, lattice models, and physics domains. Preliminary multiphase and active matter modules demonstrate the code's potential for future applications in interfacial dynamics and soft matter systems.

This work provides a validated, production-ready implementation of ELBM suitable for challenging flow simulations where numerical stability is critical, comprehensive mathematical documentation of theoretical foundations, and practical guidance for practitioners and developers.
